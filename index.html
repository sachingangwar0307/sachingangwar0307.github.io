AtliQ Hardware — Sales & Finance Analytics

Domain: Consumer electronics · Sales & Finance

Business question: How did sales and margin change over FY2019–FY2021, which markets and products drove it, and where is the target gap?

Key findings

Revenue tripled, but margin slipped about 5 points since FY19.Net sales rose 204.5% FY20→FY21 while GM% fell from 41.43% to 36.43%. Growth is costing margin; I’d check pricing and discount terms first.
Nov–Dec is the sales peak, every year.Nov–Dec are the two largest months in FY19, FY20 and FY21. Plan stock and promotions around it.
Every top market missed its target.The aggregate gap is 9.2%; Poland (18.2%), Canada (14.5%) and Spain (14.2%) are widest, India narrowest among big markets (5.9%).
Customer concentration.Amazon alone is $82.09M of FY21 sales (13.7%), then Atliq Exclusive ($61.12M) and Atliq e Store ($52.98M).
New products added value fast.The top-10 products reach $51.99M in FY21 from $6.43M in FY20 (+708%).

SQL approach

-- Fiscal year starts in September
CREATE FUNCTION get_fiscal_year(d DATE) RETURNS INT DETERMINISTIC
RETURN YEAR(DATE_ADD(d, INTERVAL 4 MONTH));

-- net_sales view: gross → pre-invoice → post-invoice deductions
(1 - post_invoice_discount_pct) * net_invoice_sales AS net_sales

Built reusable layers: functions (fiscal year, quarter), views (gross_sales → sales_postinv_discount → net_sales), 9 stored procedures (top-N markets, customers, products; market badge) and 20 numbered reports using CTEs, DENSE_RANK() and forecast-accuracy temp tables.

Recommendations

  • Investigate discount and cost drivers behind falling GM% before pushing more volume.
  • Reduce reliance on Amazon through the next-largest accounts.
  • Pair stock planning with the Nov–Dec peak.
Limitations: All figures are in USD, as in my Power BI dashboard. Some of my Excel report headers say INR, but the numbers match the USD reports. The dashboard’s FY22 estimate view is not used above. Repository: GitHub.

Data cleaning

  • Removed 6 capacity outliers (e.g. 123 bookings on 26 rooms) and invalid guest counts (negative).

Key findings

Revenue fell sharply after Q1 2020.From $4.97M in Q1 2020 to $1.15M in Q4 2020. The data doesn’t say why; I’d ask for stock, pricing and store-closure data before drawing a cause.
Online is the second-largest channel.$11.40M (about 20%) sits in the “Online” store, behind the US stores at $23.76M.
Customers come back.7,272 of 11,887 customers ordered more than once (61.2%).

SQL approach

-- Top-10 customer revenue concentration
WITH ranked AS (SELECT customer_key, revenue,
  ROW_NUMBER() OVER (ORDER BY revenue DESC) AS rnk FROM customer_sales)
SELECT SUM(CASE WHEN rnk <= 10 THEN revenue END) / SUM(revenue) * 100 FROM ranked;

42 numbered queries across sales, profitability, customers, products, stores and window-function reports (MoM and YoY growth, running totals, store ranking).

Limitations: The Excel dashboard’s default KPI cards ($18.26M revenue, $10.70M profit) are filtered to 2019; the figures above are all years unless stated. Charts use my recomputation from the workbook’s raw_sales sheet. Repository: GitHub.

Key findings

Deposits follow balances, not income.Deposits correlate with checking (r = 0.84) and savings (r = 0.75), while income correlates only weakly with loans and deposits (r = 0.26–0.33).
Upgrade and cross-sell room.Most clients sit in Jade and hold one card, so tier upgrades and second-card offers are the obvious experiments.
